# Combat and retreat

## Sub-features

Boot has two owned armies, an autonomous six-unit enemy, and distinct damageable friendly/enemy HQs. Each group includes frontline, ranged and siege. Q at the cursor requests targeted Attack on a live hostile unit or enemy HQ, or attack-location on reachable floor. The legacy `combat` assertion scenario explicitly removes the commander from its own fresh world to preserve its bounded encounter; use `strategy`/`match-win`/`match-loss` for active enemy behavior and HQ outcomes. Frontline, ranged and siege have increasing effective range; damage, death, order/target state and health are server-owned. Attack pursuit is limited to 1050 Unreal units from its destination. Move does not pursue, Hold remains position-bound, and Retreat cancels combat and heads home. Source: `ArmyGroup.cpp`, `ArmyUnit.cpp`, `CommandGameMode.cpp`, `CommandPlayerController.cpp`, `CommandHUD.cpp`, `ArmyCombatTests.cpp`.

## Primary proof and selection

Run `"$V" --run "$LOGIC_RUN" regression --scenario combat` first, using a current editor build and a new evidence directory. Require exit zero and the exact `CoopRTS.Combat.Encounter` Success result in `combat.log`. It exercises role-specific weapon range/damage, real AI attacks, target escape and death, Move/Hold/Retreat transitions, and stale-target clearing on authoritative standalone actors. This is not a network-security test or packaged Q-input test.

For a new encounter/full baseline, cover both Q modes and the combat presentation below. For a rule-only correction, run the relevant live assertions and add only unproven input/rendering checks. Binding changes require real Q input; effects/HUD changes require visual inspection. Reuse unchanged visual evidence with its build/run identified. Do not replay every capture to prove a numeric combat rule.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Launch a fresh packaged Boot. Select 1, then Space to center the selected army. From the screenshot locate the opposing orange army toward positive X/Y; pan with WASD (A still pans left), drag or zoom until the opposing units and usable floor are visible. Use 2/Space if a second friendly army is better positioned. Select only your own army. Never infer a hostile hit from a fixed screen fraction: identify an actual living unit body in the current capture.

## Targeted desktop probes

1. Capture `combat-before`: inspect both teams, the role/HP entries of the selected army and the HUD's Q control. Move toward the encounter using a right-click on verified traversable floor. Capture `combat-approach`; confirm units travel and their health is visible. Do not point into the obstacle, HUD, or a unit for a ground order.
2. Use `point --x <fraction> --y <fraction>` on an observed live hostile body (this emits pointer motion without a click), then `key q`. Capture `combat-targeted` promptly: HUD says `ATTACK TARGET`, target role/HP/distance and `Pursuit: limited to 1050 from destination`; red target/destination rings mark the accepted target. Check `actions.jsonl` and `LogArmyOrders` for an accepted Attack, not only a key event. Rejection feedback is yellow; a rejection is not an Attack pass.
3. Inspect combat across a short interval: hostile HP falls, attacks render, and a casualty changes/disappears rather than merely changing the order label. Retain only captures establishing distinct observations; avoid repeated frames of the same state. Check role silhouettes, health/target readability, and ranged/siege effects if those changed. Exact range, damage, death and pursuit limits come from live assertions, not these images.
4. Place the pointer on reachable floor using `point --x <fraction> --y <fraction>`, then `key q`. Inspect `ATTACK AREA`, its red destination ring and the absence of stale unit-target feedback. A click on a hostile body does not cover location input. Use the live scenario for numeric pursuit escape; do not recreate its entire boundary experiment through screenshots unless diagnosing a packaged-only difference.
5. Reacquire a live target if available, then right-click clear floor to replace it with Move. Capture `combat-move`: HUD says Move, green destination ring, old red target indication gone. Check movement follows the ground route without chasing the hostile. Press H near the enemy, capture `combat-hold` and `combat-held-later` after at least one second: cyan Hold state; positions remain near their assigned Hold locations although units within weapon range may fire.
6. Request Attack again if a target remains, then press R while engaged. Capture `combat-retreat` and `combat-retreat-later`: orange Retreat destination, no active target, no attack effects from retreating units and progress toward home. Keep observing after at least one weapon interval (siege is slower than frontline). Do not infer all members arrived from a center indicator or the absence of effects in one frame.

## Gotchas

`point` updates the live pointer position without changing selection or orders; unlike `click left`, it does not clear selection. `key q` uses the cursor's current hit; always capture and inspect before and after. Targeting an owned/dead unit or friendly HQ rejects rather than converting to area Attack; an empty miss/no selected army also rejects. A static-mesh floor hit retains area Attack. An enemy killed during a sequence cannot be re-targeted; choose another live unit or restart with a new run directory. A successful order log is not proof of an actual hit, bounded movement or retreat suppression. The isolated `combat` scenario does not assert enemy planning or HQ victory; use [match](match.md) for those paths. Record screenshots, `actions.jsonl`, `game.log`, and regression logs in the run's coverage note; do not mark any unrun step verified.

If the selected army loses its last member before `key r`, a rejected order is not evidence of failed disengagement: inspect the HUD's surviving count and `LogArmyOrders` before claiming a Retreat result. In the 2026-09-29 effects capture, the late R was rejected after all six died; earlier live sessions and `CoopRTS.Combat.Encounter` exercised accepted Retreat. See `Saved/Verification/combat-effects-20260929/RESULTS.md`.

Do not count an off-screen army's HUD-only capture as visual proof of travel. Pair accepted Retreat with live position/attack-suppression assertions or an observation where the members are visible. `combat-independent-20260929/RESULTS.md` records that distinction and the combat test's `PursuitRadius + 200` position tolerance; its success is not a zero-tolerance geometric proof.
