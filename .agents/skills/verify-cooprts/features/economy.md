# Capture, territory and construction economy

Each fresh commander begins with 600 resources and baseline +2/sec; the enemy has its own budget earning +2/sec per human commander (counting at least one) plus an unscaled +12/sec per established enemy sector. Payments occur every 2 seconds. Resource sectors start neutral: three on Boot, eight on Availability Zone. Occupying a sector with living units can capture it; **bare capture grants shared build rights but no income bonus**. Barracks/workshops fit within the HQ's 900-unit territory or a controlled sector's 1000-unit territory, allowing for footprint; no outpost is required and contention blocks placement. A paid, completed, living outpost establishes an owned sector: each friendly wallet gains +12/sec per established site and capture is locked without troop babysitting. Destruction removes establishment/income/capture lock, not build rights while the sector remains friendly. Buildings cost barracks 220, outpost 160 and workshop 190. Each barracks locks one typed force on first Start: capacities 6/4/2, individual prices 20/30/50 for Frontline/Ranged/Siege; Siege also charges 180 once for configuration. Replacements charge the same per-unit price on successful deployment, not a shared squad slot or manual purchase. Workshop specialization costs 150 once per owner. `CapturePoint.cpp`, `CommandGameState.cpp`, `CommandBuilding.cpp`, `CommandBuildingProduction.cpp` and `CommandPlayerState.cpp` own these rules.

## Primary live and network selection

`regression --scenario construction` (`CoopRTS.Construction.Lifecycle`) checks paid placement/rejection/refunds, owner isolation, locked configuration, real capture, bare-capture zero bonus and paid barracks placement/refund, completed outpost, no-occupant persistence, a constants-based income tick and destruction removing establishment while retaining controlled-sector build rights; costs come from the building definitions in `DA_MatchContent`, and the outpost/hostile positions derive from the sector and HQ actors. Its second controller/PlayerState is **in the same world**, not independent replication. `production` checks real per-unit prices, automatic paid casualties, independent force capacities, starvation and pause; `strategy` checks the enemy wallet and expansion; `rules` checks territory/outpost decisions and economy arithmetic without a world. `network.py --scenario economy --mode editor --clients 1` observes, on fresh host+remote, natural capture without an income bonus, a paid outpost establishing the sector after departure, owner-scoped once-only research, HQ weapon damage and fixture victory; `ownership` covers the 220 charge and rejected placements. `--scenario construction` chains them with explicit budget/capture/income fixtures.

For native proof, build a barracks, enable paid production and assign its Secure front inside a resource sector. Observe actual occupant capture. Bare capture must leave the site income bonus at zero while permitting a paid barracks/workshop in the controlled sector without an outpost. Place and complete an outpost in that friendly uncontested sector, compare wallet/income (+12/sec per friendly wallet above +2/sec baseline, paid every 2 seconds), then move the barracks front away and inspect continued establishment without occupants. Observe a real enemy attack for destruction: income/capture lock stop, but friendly control still grants build rights until recapture and contention still blocks placement. Pair images with action/game logs, and use live/socket assertions for atomic server rejection and independent replica convergence. Earlier economy evidence predates this quick-playtest balance and is not proof of the new rates/rights.

Inspect native captures of neutral, partially friendly/enemy captured and fully owned sectors: `SECTOR n` uses gold/green/red owner-coloured canvas text with an 88×4 HUD-pixel capture bar. The fill is `abs(CaptureProgress)`, green for positive/friendly progress and red for negative/enemy progress; label colour follows the current owner while recapture drains its fill. Check both zoom extremes, 1280×720 and 2560×1440, and modal/off-screen hiding. The quick HUD deck capture covers only visible baseline sectors, not these capture transitions.
